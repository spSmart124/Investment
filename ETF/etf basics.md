# Basics of Exchange Traded Funds
## Active Investing
* Active investing involves a hands-on approach by the portfolio manager
* Investments are selected based on an independent assessment of their worth, aimed at choosing the most attractive investments
* The goal of active managers is to provide higher returns by 'beating the market' or outperforming certain benchmark indices
* Active investing style also involves higher risk, and has higher fees than passive investing

## Passive Investing
* Investment strategy that mirrors a benchmark index
* Fund manager usually tracks a benchmark index and tries to provide a return in line with the benchmark index
* No active buying and selling
* Characterized by low fees
* Index funds and ETFs follow a passive investment strategy

## Index Funds
* An index fund is a portfolio of stocks or bonds
* Designed to mimic the composition and performance of a financial market index
* A broad-based equity index fund tracks a benchmark index like the Nifty50
* They follow a passive investment strategy, therefore has low fees
* They seek to match the risk and return of the market

## Exchange Traded Funds
* An ETF is similar to an Index Fund that mimics a benchmark Index
* ETF prices fluctuate all day
* Provides investors exposure to broad market segments
* ETF units can be traded on an exchange like a stock
* Low expense ratio

## Index Fund vs ETFs
| Index Funds | Exchange Traded Funds |
| ----------- | --------------------- |
| An Index Fund is a Mutual funds that tracks and mimics the composition of indices like the Nifty50, Sensex, or global indices | ETFs also do the same thing and track a benchmark index, like the Nifty50, Sensex, or global indices |
| Index fund units are bought and sold directly with the mutual fund | ETF units can be traded (bought and sold) on the exchange like stocks during market hours |
| Index Funds are priced at the close of the trading day based on the NAV of the underlying securities | ETFs are priced to the market throughout the trading day |
| Index funds do not require a demat account | ETFs require demat and trading accounts because they are exchange tradeable units |
| Index funds are passively managed, hence have low fees | ETFs are passively managed, hence have low fees |
| You can also invest in Index funds that track the prices of commodities like gold, crude oil etc. | You can also invest in ETFs that track the prices of commodities like gold, crude oil etc. |

## How ETFs Work
* ETFs track indices, such as the Nifty 50, BSE Sensex, etc.
* When you buy ETF units, you buy shares or units of a portfolio, that tracks the yield and return of its native index.
* ETFs don't try to outperform their corresponding index, but simply replicate the performance of the Index.
* They don't try to beat the market; they try to be the market.

## Types of ETFs
* Equity ETFs
    * Broad Market Index ETF (Nifty 50)
    * Sector ETF (Nifty Bank)
    * Thematic ETF (Nifty Housing Index)
* Debt ETFs
    * Constant Debt ETF
    * Target Maturity Debt ETF
* Commodity ETF
    * Gold ETF

### Equity ETFs
* Equity ETFs are investment vehicles that combine the flexibility of investing in stocks with the simplicity of mutual funds.
* The holdings of the ETF are completely transparent due to its index mirroring nature.
* ETFs, like stocks, trade on the stock exchanges and you can buy and sell them anytime during the trading hours.
* Equity Exchange Traded Funds also have a lower expense ratio than mutual funds due to their unique structure and portfolio construction mechanism.
* Equity ETFs are index-based passive investment schemes that invest in securities in the same proportion as the underlying index.

#### Broad Market Index ETF
* A Broad Market Index ETF is an ETF that comprise of a basket of stocks that track a benchmark stock index, like the Nifty50
* The aim is to provide a diversified exposure to the stock market

#### Sector ETF
A sector ETF is a pooled investment vehicle that invests specifically in the stocks and securities of a particular industry or sector, typically identified in the fund's title.
For instance, a sector ETF may track a representative basket of energy stocks or technology stocks.

#### Thematic ETF
* A thematic ETF is a fund that offers the opportunity to invest based on a particular theme, such as climate change or artificial intelligence.
* The ETF then holds companies that should benefit from that trend.
* While traditional ETFs are often based on a broad market index where investors can achieve diversification at a low cost, you likely won't be sufficiently diversified just by owning a thematic ETF, because the companies' fortunes will be heavily tied to the same underlying trend.

### Debt ETFs
* Debt ETFs invest in Debt Instruments and are listed on exchange.
* These Funds are passively managed Funds which follow a specific benchmark debt Index
* Investors get units of ETFs which are listed and traded on the Exchange
* Investors can buy or sell units of ETF on the exchange any time during the market hours

SEBI directive on categorization of Debt ETFs based on composition

Mutual fund houses that launch Debt ETFs or Index Funds should categorize them based on the following indices:
* Corporate Debt Securities under Corporate debt indices
* Government Securities (G-sec), T-bills, and/or State Development Loans (SDLs) under G-sec indices
* A combination of Corporate Debt Securities and G-sec/T-bills/SDLs under Hybrid debt indices

#### Target Maturity Debt ETF
* A Target Maturity Debt ETF has a defined fixed maturity and invests in debt securities with similar maturity
* This enables it to combine features of debt securities and mutual funds
* In these ETFs, the underlying securities are held till the maturity of the scheme

#### Constant Debt ETF
* Unlike a Target Maturity Date ETF, a Constant Debt ETF does not have a defined maturity date

### Commodity ETF
* Commodity ETFs invest in commodities, including crude oil or gold.
* The most popular commodity ETFs in India are Gold ETFs.
* Investing in Gold ETFs is more convenient than investing in physical gold.

#### Gold ETF
* A Gold ETF aims to track the domestic physical gold price. They are passive investment instruments that are based on gold prices and invest in gold bullion.
* Gold ETFs units represent physical gold which may be in paper or dematerialized form. One Gold ETF unit is equal to 1 gram of gold and is backed by physical gold of very high purity.

## Investment Concepts
### Risk Tolerance
* Risk tolerance is a measure of the degree of loss an investor is willing to endure within their portfolio
* Stock volatility, market swings, economic or political events, and regulatory, or interest rate changes affect an investor's tolerance for risk
* Age, investment goals, and income contribute to an investor's risk tolerance
* An aggressive investor
* commonly has a higher risk tolerance and is willing to risk more money for the possibility of better, yet unknown, returns
* A conservative investor commonly has a lower risk tolerance and seeks investments with guaranteed returns

### Portfolio
* A portfolio is a collection of financial investments like stocks, bonds, commodities, cash, and cash equivalents, as well as their fund counterparts.
* Stocks and bonds are generally considered a portfolio's core building blocks, though you may grow a portfolio with many different types of assets-including real estate, gold, paintings, and other art collectibles.

### Asset Class
* An asset class is a group of similar investment vehicles. The main asset classes are equity, debt, cash, and gold.
* Different classes, or types, of investment assets, such as fixed-income investments, are grouped together based on having a similar financial structure
* They are typically traded in the same financial markets and subject to the same rules and regulations
