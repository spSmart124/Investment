# Basics of Exchange Traded Funds
## Active Investing
* Active investing involves a hands-on approach by the portfolio manager
* Investments are selected based on an independent assessment of their worth, aimed at choosing the most attractive investments
* The goal of active managers is to provide higher returns by 'beating the market' or outperforming certain benchmark indices
* Active investing style also involves higher risk, and has higher fees than passive investing

## Passive Investing
* Investment strategy that mirrors a benchmark index
* Fund manager usually tracks a benchmark index and tries to provide a return in line with the benchmark index
* No active buying and selling
* Characterized by low fees
* Index funds and ETFs follow a passive investment strategy

## Index Funds
* An index fund is a portfolio of stocks or bonds
* Designed to mimic the composition and performance of a financial market index
* A broad-based equity index fund tracks a benchmark index like the Nifty50
* They follow a passive investment strategy, therefore has low fees
* They seek to match the risk and return of the market

## Exchange Traded Funds
* An ETF is similar to an Index Fund that mimics a benchmark Index
* ETF prices fluctuate all day
* Provides investors exposure to broad market segments
* ETF units can be traded on an exchange like a stock
* Low expense ratio

## Index Fund vs ETFs

## How ETFs Work
* ETFs track indices, such as the Nifty 50, BSE Sensex, etc.
* When you buy ETF units, you buy shares or units of a portfolio, that tracks the yield and return of its native index.
* ETFs don't try to outperform their corresponding index, but simply replicate the performance of the Index.
* They don't try to beat the market; they try to be the market.

### Equity ETFs
* Equity ETFs are investment vehicles that combine the flexibility of investing in stocks with the simplicity of mutual funds.
* The holdings of the ETF are completely transparent due to its index mirroring nature.
* ETFs, like stocks, trade on the stock exchanges and you can buy and sell them anytime during the trading hours.
* Equity Exchange Traded Funds also have a lower expense ratio than mutual funds due to their unique structure and portfolio construction mechanism.
* Equity ETFs are index-based passive investment schemes that invest in securities in the same proportion as the underlying index.

#### Broad Market Index ETF
* A Broad Market Index ETF is an ETF that comprise of a basket of stocks that track a benchmark stock index, like the Nifty50
* The aim is to provide a diversified exposure to the stock market

#### Sector ETF
A sector ETF is a pooled investment vehicle that invests specifically in the stocks and securities of a particular industry or sector, typically identified in the fund's title.
For instance, a sector ETF may track a representative basket of energy stocks or technology stocks.

#### Thematic ETF
* A thematic ETF is a fund that offers the opportunity to invest based on a particular theme, such as climate change or artificial intelligence.
* The ETF then holds companies that should benefit from that trend.
* While traditional ETFs are often based on a broad market index where investors can achieve diversification at a low cost, you likely won't be sufficiently diversified just by owning a thematic ETF, because the companies' fortunes will be heavily tied to the same underlying trend.

### Debt ETFs
* Debt ETFs invest in Debt Instruments and are listed on exchange.
* These Funds are passively managed Funds which follow a specific benchmark debt Index
* Investors get units of ETFs which are listed and traded on the Exchange
* Investors can buy or sell units of ETF on the exchange any time during the market hours

SEBI directive on categorization of Debt ETFs based on composition

Mutual fund houses that launch Debt ETFs or Index Funds should categorize them based on the following indices:
* Corporate Debt Securities under Corporate debt indices
* Government Securities (G-sec), T-bills, and/or State Development Loans (SDLs) under G-sec indices
* A combination of Corporate Debt Securities and G-sec/T-bills/SDLs under Hybrid debt indices

#### Target Maturity Debt ETF
* A Target Maturity Debt ETF has a defined fixed maturity and invests in debt securities with similar maturity
* This enables it to combine features of debt securities and mutual funds
* In these ETFs, the underlying securities are held till the maturity of the scheme

#### Constant Debt ETF
* Unlike a Target Maturity Date ETF, a Constant Debt ETF does not have a defined maturity date

### Commodity ETF
* Commodity ETFs invest in commodities, including crude oil or gold.
* The most popular commodity ETFs in India are Gold ETFs.
* Investing in Gold ETFs is more convenient than investing in physical gold.

#### Gold ETF
* A Gold ETF aims to track the domestic physical gold price. They are passive investment instruments that are based on gold prices and invest in gold bullion.
* Gold ETFs units represent physical gold which may be in paper or dematerialized form. One Gold ETF unit is equal to 1 gram of gold and is backed by physical gold of very high purity.

## Investment Concepts
### Risk Tolerance
* Risk tolerance is a measure of the degree of loss an investor is willing to endure within their portfolio
* Stock volatility, market swings, economic or political events, and regulatory, or interest rate changes affect an investor's tolerance for risk
* Age, investment goals, and income contribute to an investor's risk tolerance
* An aggressive investor
* commonly has a higher risk tolerance and is willing to risk more money for the possibility of better, yet unknown, returns
* A conservative investor commonly has a lower risk tolerance and seeks investments with guaranteed returns

### Portfolio
* A portfolio is a collection of financial investments like stocks, bonds, commodities, cash, and cash equivalents, as well as their fund counterparts.
* Stocks and bonds are generally considered a portfolio's core building blocks, though you may grow a portfolio with many different types of assets-including real estate, gold, paintings, and other art collectibles.

### Asset Class
* An asset class is a group of similar investment vehicles. The main asset classes are equity, debt, cash, and gold.
* Different classes, or types, of investment assets, such as fixed-income investments, are grouped together based on having a similar financial structure
* They are typically traded in the same financial markets and subject to the same rules and regulations

### Diversification
**Do not put all your eggs in one basket** is a time-tested investment maxim. Diversification is the technique of reducing risk, by spreading your investments across different asset classes, industries, and other categories.

* Diversification is an investing strategy used to manage investment risk.
* Rather than concentrate money in a single company, industry, sector or asset class, investors diversify their investments across a range of different companies, industries and asset classes.
* When you divide your funds across companies large and small, in both equity and debt, across industries and sectors, you avoid the risk of having all your eggs in one basket.
* Since the future is highly uncertain and markets are always changing, we diversify our investments among different companies and assets that are not exposed to the same risks.

### Asset Allocation
* Asset allocation is an investment strategy that aims to balance risk and reward by apportioning a portfolio's assets according to an individual's goals, risk tolerance, and investment horizon
* The three main asset classes-equities, fixed-income, and cash and equivalents-have different levels of risk and return, so each will behave differently over time
* There is no simple formula that can find the right asset allocation for every individual. It depends on many factors such as investor's age, investment horizon, risk appetite etc.

### Exchange tradability
* An Index Fund unit is not exchange tradable. Whereas an ETF unit is exchange tradable, just like an equity share
* An exchange tradeable asset can be bought or sold on a financial exchange during market hours. A tradeable asset has a market price that fluctuates throughout trading hours

### Liquidity
* Liquidity is the combination of two factors - marketability and price continuity.
* Marketable assets are assets that can be converted into cash quickly.
* Price continuity is the characteristic of a liquid market where price movements between transactions are relatively small.
* A higher trade volume indicates a greater overall market interest for shares that get traded more frequently and more rapidly than the ones with lower volume. Hence, a high trade volume is generally an indication of a high liquidity level for a particular security or commodity in the market.

## Mutual Fund Basics
* A lot of people pool their money
* The money is managed by a professional fund manager
* The money is invested in equities, bonds, money market instruments or other securities
* Each investor owns units, which represent a portion of the holdings of the fund


* Investors pool funds
* Fund manager invests the pooled funds on behalf of the investors
* The pooled fund is invested into a basket of securities also called a portfolio

### Mutual fund valuation
* A mutual fund unit is valued at the end of each market day, based on the closing market prices of the portfolio's securities
* The NAV is the per-unit market value of a mutual fund

The performance of a particular scheme of a Mutual Fund is denoted by its Net Asset Value (NAV).

```
NAV = 
Market value of securities held by the mutual fund scheme / Total number of units of the scheme on any particular date
```

Since, market value of the securities change daily the NAV of a Mutual Fund Scheme varies day to day.

### Types of Mutual Funds
* Equity Fund
* Debt Fund
* Hybrid Fund
* Gold ETF

#### Equity Fund
* An equity fund is a mutual fund scheme that invests predominantly in equity stocks.
* In the Indian context, as per current SEBI Mutual Fund Regulations, an equity mutual fund scheme must invest at least 65% of the scheme's assets in equities and equity related instruments.
* An Equity Fund can be actively managed or passively managed. Index funds and ETFs are passively managed.

#### Debt Fund
* A debt fund is a mutual fund scheme that invests in fixed income instruments, such as Corporate and Government Bonds, Corporate Debt Securities, and Money Market Instruments etc. that offer capital appreciation. Debt funds are also referred to as Income Funds or Bond Funds.
* Debt funds are ideal for investors who want regular income but are risk-averse. Debt funds are less volatile and, hence, are less risky than equity funds.

#### Hybrid Fund
* A hybrid fund combines equity stock component, a bond component and sometimes a money market component in a single portfolio.
* These funds invest in a mix of equities and debt, giving the investor the best of both worlds.
* Hybrid funds gain from a healthy dose of equities, but the debt portion fortifies them against any downturn.

#### Gold ETF
* A Gold ETF is an exchange-traded fund (ETF) that aims to track the domestic physical gold price. They are passive investment instruments that are based on gold prices and invest in gold bullion.
* In short, Gold ETFs are units representing physical gold which may be in paper or dematerialized form.
* One Gold ETF unit is equal to 1 gram of gold and is backed by physical gold of very high purity. Gold ETFs combine the flexibility of stock investment and the simplicity of gold investments.

### Structure of Mutual Funds
* Open-ended fund: Investors can invest or withdraw from the fund anytime
* Close-ended fund: Investors can withdraw invested amount only on maturity or at fixed dates

## Stock Index
An index
* Usually made up of a set number of the top shares from a given exchange
* An equity index moves in points and reflects the prices of its underlying stocks
* Some stock indices give equal weights to its constituent stocks. Others may give more weightage to larger stocks

Nifty 50 is the flagship index of Nation Stock Exchange of India (NSE). The Index tracks the behavior of a portfolio of blue-chip companies, the largest and most liquid Indian securities. Includes 50 of the approximately 1600 companies traded on the NSE. It captures approximately 65% of it's market capitalization. It is a true reflection of indian stock market.

The NIFTY 50 covers major sectors of the Indian economy and offers investment managers exposure to the Indian market in one efficient portfolio. The Index has been trading since April 1996 and is well suited for benchmarking, index funds and index-based derivatives.

### Structure of an ETF

![Structure of ETF](../resources/structure%20of%20etf.png)

#### ETF creation process
* The ETF creation process begins when a prospective ETF Asset Management Company (known as a sponsor) files a plan with SEBI to create an ETF.
* The sponsor then forms an agreement with an authorized participant, generally a market maker, specialist, or large institutional investor.
* The authorized participant acquires shares, places those shares in a trust, and uses them to form ETF creation units-bundles of stock varying from 10,000 to shares.
* The trust provides shares of the ETF, which are legal claims on the shares held in the trust (the ETFS represent tiny slivers of the creation units), to the authorized participant.
* Once the authorized participant receives the ETF shares, they are sold to the public on the open market just like stock shares.

#### Redeeming an ETF - Two ways
* The first is to sell the shares on the open market. This is generally the option chosen by most individual investors.
* The second is to gather enough shares of the ETF to form a creation unit, then exchange the creation unit for the underlying securities. This option is generally only available to institutional investors due to the large number of shares required to form a creation unit. When these investors redeem their shares, the creation unit is destroyed, and the securities are turned over to the redeemer. The beauty of this option is in its tax implications for the portfolio.

#### FAQ on ETF
1. Are ETFs cost-efficient?
    * An ETF tracks an index without trying to outperform it, it incurs lower administrative and management costs than actively managed portfolios.
    * Typical ETF administrative and management costs are lower than an actively managed fund, coming in less than 0.20% per annum, as opposed to the over 1% yearly cost of some actively managed mutual fund schemes.
    * They have lower expense ratio, it adds to the total return to the investor.

1. What are the costs of investing in ETFs? While the Expense Ratio of ETFs is lower, there are certain costs that are unique to ETFs.
    * ETFs are traded on stock exchange through a stockbroker, every time an investor makes a purchase or sale, he/she pays a brokerage for the transaction.
    * In addition, an investor may also incur Securities Transaction Tax and the usual costs of trading in stocks, including differences in the ask-bid spread etc.
    * Traditional Mutual Fund investors are also subjected to the same trading costs indirectly, as the Fund in turn pays for these costs.

1. Are ETFs only for stocks?
    * No. Any asset class that has a published index and is liquid enough to be traded daily can be made into an ETF.
    * Bonds, real estate, commodities, currencies, and multi-asset funds are all available in an ETF format.
    * For instance, Mutual Funds in India offer Gold ETFs, where the underlying investment is in physical gold.

1. What happens to the dividends of the underlying stocks?
    * Dividends received by an ETF are typically reinvested in the Fund.

1. What happens if the underlying stocks in the index change?
    * The underlying stocks in the index may be changed as and when securities in the index do not match specific criteria laid down by the index service provider or a better candidate is available to replace a constituent.
    * The index service provider usually makes announcements of change well in advance. Once securities in the underlying index are changed, the Fund can change the securities in its underlying portfolio by selling the securities that are being removed from the index and including those that are included in the index.
    * This will in no way affect the units being held by an investor, as the units will continue to track the Index.

1. What is meant by tracking difference?
    * Tracking difference is the return difference between a passive ETF and the index that it is tracking over a certain period.
    * It measures the difference of change in net asset value of the passive ETF and change in the index that it is tracking over a certain period.

1. Won't ETFs also be affected by market changes?
    * ETFs listed on a stock exchange are subject to price fluctuations as per market trends.
    * Earning a profit or incurring a loss depends heavily on the stock market conditions

### Benefits of investing in ETFs over mutual funds
* A diversified pool of securities. Purchasing shares of a company keeps you limited to the performance of the company itself, subjecting you to a higher degree of risk.
* On the other hand, investing in ETFs allow you to keep your finance spread over equities of different companies. You get exposure to 50 stocks if your invest in an ETF that track the Nifty50
* Even if one asset underperforms in the pool of resources in an ETF, it can be compensated by the positive performance of other assets
* One of the significant benefits of investing in ETFs over mutual funds is the reduced expenses
* There are various charges involved in mutual funds, such as entry and exit load, management fees etc. This increases your total cost incurred, and thereby the total expense ratio of mutual funds
* As ETFs are traded like shares in the stock market, their expense ratio is considerably lower

### Tradability and Liquidity
* Any change in the value of an ETF can be observed instantly and can be bought and sold throughout the business day
* ETFs therefore have much higher liquidity than mutual funds. This enables you to have flexibility in your choices of investing, allowing you to shift to another security with ease in case a particular asset is not generating adequate profits. You can do this intra-day

### Passively managed
* Investing in ETFs is generally less risky as they are passively managed. They only invest in the best performing companies listed in a particular stock exchange, while mutual funds thoroughly assess all the businesses with a potential for growth
* This subjects mutual funds to a greater risk as newly formed small scale companies have higher chances of incurring a loss

### Demat account and trading knowledge
* Since ETFs are traded like shares, there are several expenses that must be incurred to purchase them
* A demat account must be opened to buy and sell ETF units. Operating a demat account requires a basic knowledge of the stock market and its associated techniques, which might be difficult for new investors
