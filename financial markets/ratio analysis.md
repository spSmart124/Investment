# Ratio Analysis
## Liquidity Ratio
The ratio that can be used to determine short term liquidity position of a company i.e the liquidity within the span of a year are
1. Current Ratio
2. Acid Test or Quick Ratio
3. Turnover Ratio

These ratios depend on the current asset and current liabilities of the company.

### Current Ratio
**Current Ratio** indicates ability of a company to meet it's current liabilities from it's current assets. It is calculated as

```
Current Ratio = Current Asset / Current Liabilities
```

### Acid Test Ratio
A high value is an indicator of good short term solvenacy.

```
Acid Test Ratio = Quick Assets / Current Liabilities
```

```
Quick Assets = Current Assets - Inventories and Incurred Expenses
```

The Acid Test Ratio is a measurement of firm's ability to convert it's current assets quickly into cash to meet its current liabilities. A 1:1 ratio is generally considered satisfactory.

### Turnover Ratio
Turnover Ratio measures how quickly certain current assets are converted into cash. Some important turnover ratio are
* Inventory Turnover Ratio
* Debtors Turnover Ratio
* Average Collection Period
* Fixed Assets Turnover
* Total Assets Turnover

```
Inventory Turnover Ratio = Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory
```

It indicates the efficiency of inventory management. Higher value means higher efficiency.

```
Cost of Goods = Net Sales - Gross Profit
```

```
Cost of Average Inventory = (Opening Stock + Closing Stock) / 2
```

### Debtors Turnover Ratio
The Debtors Turnover Ratio is used to determine how quickly the company is able extend credit to it's customers. High debtors turnover ratio is an indication of high efficiency is collecting debts. 

```
Debtors' Turnover Ratio = Net Credit Sales / Average Accounts Receivable (Debtors)
```

### Average Collection Period
Average Collection Period represents the no of days for which a sale takes to be paid by debtors.

```
Average Collection Period = Average Debtors / Average Daily Credit Sales
```

It can also be calculated as

```
Average Collection Period = 365 / Debtors Turnover Ratio
```

### Fixed Assets Turnover
The efficient use of Fixed Assets is determined by Fixed Assets Turnover which measures sales per rupee of investment in fixed assets.

```
Fixed Assets Turnover Ratio = Net Sales / Net Fixed Assets
```

### Total Assets Turnover Ratio

```
Total Assets Turnover Ratio = Net Sales / Average Total Assets
```

Total Assets Turnover Ratio is a measure of how efficiently all types of assets are employed.

## Leverage Ratio
Long term solvenacy of a firm which is its ability to repay debts in different forms including loan amount along with interest can be judged using **Leverage or Capital Structure Ratio**.

### Balance Sheet Based Ratio
Balance Sheet Based Ratio helps determine relationship between borrowed funds and owners capitals. In this type, Debt to Equity Ratio reflects relative contribution of creditors and owners in financing the business.

```
Debt-Equity Ratio = Total Debt / Total Equity
```

```
Debt-Asset Ratio = Total Debt / Total Assets
```

### Coverage Ratio
Ratio derived from firm's Profit & Loss Account, also called Coverage Ratio, measures the relationship between the proceeds from the operations of the firm and the claims of outsiders.

Interest Coverage Ratio is used by lenders to assess the capacity of a firm. It is calculated as.

```
Interest Coverage Ratio = Earnings before Interest & Taxes / Interest
```

A high Interest Coverage Ratio indicates a better interest payment capacity of the firm.

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) is a better indicator of a firm's ability to serve it's debt. Financial institutions uses this for the period during which the term loan of a project is repayable.

```
DSCR = Profit after Tax + Depreciation + Other Non-cash Expenditure + Interest on Term Loan / Interest on Term Loan + Repayment of Term Loan
```

## Profitability Ratio
Important ratios that can be used to measure profitability are
* Gross Profit Ratio
* Net Profit Ratio

```
Gross Profit Ratio = Gross Profit X 100 / Net Sales
```

```
Net Profit Ratio = Net Profit X 100 / Net Sales
```

### Investment Related Profitability Ratio
```
Return on Total Assets = Profit Before Interest and Tax / Fixed Assets + Current Assets
```

```
Return on Capital Employed = Net Profit after Tax / Total Capital Employed
```

The distribution of profits or assets or liquidation are first used to meet the claims of creditors and preference shareholders. After which common shareholders or equity shareholders receive their benefits. Common shareholders' benefits are truely reflected by return on equity and earnings per share (EPS).

```
Return on Shareholder's Equity = Net Profit After Tax X 100 / Average Total Shareholder's Equity or Net Worth
```

Net worth includes Shareholders' equity capital plus reserves and surplus.

```
Earnings per Share or EPS = Net Profit Available to the Shareholder / Number of Ordinary Shares Outstanding
```

```
Price Earnings Ratio = Market Price per Share / EPS
```
