# Trend Analysis
## Introduction
Markets tend to move in a particular direction or trend.

* Market moving in a downward direction
* Market moving in an upward direction

As an analyst, it is very important to identify these trends, and the attributes of the trend.

### Market Noise
Charles Dow believed that trends existed despite 'market noise'.
Let us take an example of NIFTY index to illustrate this:

![Market Noise](../resources/market%20noise.png)

Market Noises or Corrections are temporary price declines, interrupting an uptrend. This means that markets might temporarily move in the direction opposite to the trend, but will soon resume their prior move.

### Trend Reversal
Changes in price movements, which lead to a change in the current trend is called a Reversal.

![Trend Reversal](../resources/trend%20reversal.png)


Determining whether the price movement is the start of a new trend(reversal) or a temporary market noise(correction) in the current trend is not easy. This determines the success of the chartist.

## Trend Formation
A trend is formed by a 'peak' and 'trough'. The tops or the highest points in a rally is called a peak. Rally is nothing but a rise in stock prices. The bottoms or the lowest points in a rally is called a trough.

## Types of Trends
* Rising Trend
* Falling Trend
* Stable Trend
* Volatile Trend

![Types of Trend](../resources/types%20of%20trend.png)

### Rising Trend
In a rising trend, the stock price makes higher highs and higher lows successively, on a 'closing basis'.

![Rising Trend](../resources/rising%20trend.png)

If you look at the graph, Peak 2 is higher than Peak 1 and Peak 3 is higher than Peak 2. The later peak would be higher (in terms of closing price) than the earlier peak. This is referred as higher highs.

Likewise, when you look at the troughs the lows in the graph the Trough 2 is higher than Trough 1 and Trough 3 is higher than Trough 2. In other words the lows are becoming higher. The later trough would be higher (in terms of closing price) than the earlier trough.

### Falling Trend
In a falling trend, the stock price makes lower highs and lower lows on a 'closing basis'.

![Falling Trend](../resources/falling%20trend.png)

In the graph Peak 2 is lower than the Peak 1. Similarly, Through 2 is lower than Trough 1.

* Every successive peak is lower (in terms of closing price) than the earlier one.
* Every successive trough is lower (in terms of closing price) than the earlier one.

### Stable Trend
In a stable trend, the stock prices move in a narrow range - termed as 'sideways movement'. The peaks are almost equal (in terms of closing price) to the earlier peak. The troughs are almost equal (in terms of closing price) to the earlier troughs.

![Stable Trend](../resources/stable%20trend.png)

If you look at the graph, Peak 1, Peak 2 and Peak 3 are almost same. And Trough 1, Trough 2 and Trough 3 are almost same.

A Stable or neutral trend shows the market is waiting for a 'trigger', for the stock to move either way.

#### Trigger
A trigger can be a news or an event, positive or negative, pertaining to a stock, which can result in a sharp spike or fall in the stock price.

![Trigger News Snippet](../resources/trigger%20new%20snippet.png)

If you look at the above new snippet it states "3i Infotech shares zoom on IBM stake buy report". So, based on a news or event stock tend to move and that is a trigger.

##### Example
You have invested INR 50,000 in Newgen Bank stock. The following chart depicts a stable trend in the stock, over the past 3 months.

![Stable Trend Example](../resources/stable%20trend%20example.png)

If you look at the chart the peaks and troughs are almost equal for the past 3 months. A news came that says "RBI increases repo rate by 50 basis points."

Which direction do you think the prices will move?
* Stock price will move up
* Stock price will move remain stable
* Stock price will move down

The answer is "Stock price will move down".

Let's understand why. After a stable trend a news comes about RBI raising repo rate.

![Trigger Example](../resources/trigger%20example.png)

The Reserve Bank of India has raised the repo rate, the rate at which RBI lends money to banks, by 50 basis points (bps). This is a signal that cost of funds are expected to be higher, affecting profitability of Newgen Bank. This news trigger may start a new downward trend in the stock.

### Volatile Trend
In a volatile trend, the stock or an index makes higher highs and lower lows. It moves in both directions with no specific trend, generally due to some news or events pertaining to the stock.

![Volatile Trend](../resources/volatile%20trend.png)

** Important:** Volatile trends might look attractive to intraday investors. For long term investors, it is best to wait till a new trend emerges.

#### Volatility Calculation
Volatility refers to the variation in the value of the underlying stock. It is mathematically calculated as the difference in the opening and closing prices divided by the average value, expressed in percentage.

For example, a stock had a price range of INR 900 and INR 950 on a particular day.

| Time | Stock Price |
| ---- | ----------- |
| 9:30 AM | 900 |
| 10:30 AM | 920 |
| 12:00 PM | 890 |
| 13:00 PM | 940 |
| 14:30 PM | 870 |
| 15:30 PM | 950 |

```
Volatility = (Closing Price - Opening Price ) / Average of opening and closing price * 100

=> (950 - 900) / 925 * 100
```

Volatility = 5.4%
